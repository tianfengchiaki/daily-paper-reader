Abstract 

Embedding advertisements into large language model (LLM) outputs introduces a fun-damental tension: revenue optimization can distort content and degrade user experience. Existing approaches largely ignore this trade-off, often forcing irrelevant ads into responses. We propose a quality-preserving auction framework that explicitly integrates content fi-delity into the mechanism design. Built on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), our approach treats organic content as a reference and derives an endogenous reserve price that screens out ads with non-positive marginal social welfare contributions. We develop a KL-regularized single-allocation mechanism with Myerson payments and a screened VCG multi-allocation mechanism, both satisfying dominant-strategy incentive compatibility and individual rationality. Experiments across diverse scenarios demonstrate that our mecha-nisms outperform existing baselines in metrics such as revenue per ad and semantic similarity to no-ad responses. Our results establish a new paradigm for LLM advertising that enables monetization without compromising output quality. 

1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) are rapidly becoming the primary interface for information access, enabling applications such as question answering, content generation, and decision support [ 1, 4, 28 , 31 ]. Their training and deployment, however, incur substantial computa-tional cost, creating an urgent need for scalable monetization [ 23 ]. Online advertising is a natural solution [ 7]: platforms can allow advertisers to bid for inclusion in LLM-generated responses. Unlike traditional display advertising, this paradigm embeds ads directly into gen-erated content, introducing a fundamental tension between revenue maximization and output quality. Recent work proposes auction mechanisms for LLM-based advertising [ 10 , 11 , 15 , 27 ], but largely overlooks this tension. Existing approaches optimize welfare based only on advertiser value and restrict generation to sponsored content, which can introduce irrelevant ads and degrade coherence [ 11 , 15 ]. While Soumalias et al. [27] address this via distributional penalties, their method incurs inference costs that scale with the number of advertisers. A key challenge remains: how to design auction mechanisms that enable monetization without compromising the semantic fidelity of LLM outputs?

In this paper, we propose a quality-preserving mechanism design framework for LLM advertising. Our approach builds on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) [ 20 ] and treats organic content, namely the no-ad source such as Wikipedia pages or outputs from internal tools, as a reference that anchors output quality [ 14 , 18 ]. We incorporate this organic content directly into the social welfare objective, measuring quality as the semantic similarity to no-ad responses. This perspective fundamentally changes the role of the auction: instead of allocating over the full set of candidate ads, the mechanism admits only those that improve the overall quality-adjusted social welfare. A key implication of this formulation is an endogenous reserve price that screens advertis-ers based on their marginal contribution relative to the organic baseline. This screening step excludes ads that do not contribute positively to the overall quality-adjusted welfare, whether due to semantic irrelevance or low bids, thereby preserving content quality while ensuring a revenue lower bound for the platform. Compared to prior RAG-based mechanisms [ 15 ], which incorporate ads without filtering, our approach explicitly enforces quality preservation at the level of the allocation rule. Building on the screened RAG model, we design both single-allocation and multi-allocation auction mechanisms. In the single-allocation setting, we maximize a Kullback-Leibler (KL)-regularized social welfare objective that penalizes deviation from the screened RAG model, which we treat as the semantically optimal retrieval distribution, yielding a tractable allocation rule with payments derived via Myerson’s envelope formula [ 24 ], and thus achieving dominant-strategy incentive compatibility (DSIC) and individual rationality (IR). In the multi-allocation setting, we adapt the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) mechanism [ 8, 13 , 29 ] to select a welfare-optimal subset of ads. Crucially, the screening step reduces the candidate set to a small eligible subset, making the mechanism computationally efficient while preserving incentive guarantees. We evaluate our framework across diverse queries, advertiser sets, and bid profiles, and compare against the most closely related RAG-based baseline [ 15 ]. Across all scenarios, our mechanisms consistently achieve higher revenue, relevance, and social welfare, while maintaining substantially better output quality, measured as semantic similarity to no-ad responses. These results demonstrate that the proposed mechanism design establishes a new paradigm for LLM advertising that enables monetization without compromising output quality. In conclusion, our contributions are threefold. 

• We introduce a quality-preserving auction framework for LLM advertising that incorporates organic content into the social welfare objective and derives an endogenous reserve price to screen out ads with negative contributions to the quality-adjusted social welfare. 

• We design incentive-compatible single- and multi-allocation mechanisms: a KL-regularized Myerson mechanism for single allocation, and a screened VCG mechanism for multi-allocation with reduced computational complexity. 

• We provide extensive empirical validation demonstrating consistent improvements over existing RAG-based mechanisms in revenue, semantic relevance, social welfare, and output quality. 

Related Work. Our work relates to three threads of literature: (i) auction design for LLM-generated contents, (ii) RAG, and (iii) reserve prices in auction theory. 2First, mechanism design for LLM-generated content has recently emerged at the inter-section of machine learning and economics [ 12 ]. Existing approaches differ mainly in the 

granularity of allocation and how ads are integrated into generation. At the token level ,Duetting et al. [11] propose per-token bidding, but such fine-grained control does not ensure semantic coherence. At the response level , Soumalias et al. [27] aggregate multiple candidates at substantial inference cost, while Dubey et al. [10] focus on ad summarization. Balseiro et al. [3] extend position auctions to AI-generated content under known click-through rates. Most closely related are segment-level RAG-based mechanisms by Hajiaghayi et al. [15] , which integrate ads without additional inference cost but restrict generation to sponsored content, potentially introducing irrelevant ads, and lack incentive guarantees in the multi-allocation setting. In contrast, we incorporate organic content into the allocation problem, yielding a quality-preserving objective that screens out ads with negative marginal welfare contributions and supports incentive-compatible mechanisms. Second, RAG [ 20 ] enhances LLMs by conditioning outputs on retrieved documents and is widely used to improve factuality and relevance in question answering [ 14 , 18 , 30 ]. Prior work uses RAG primarily as a modeling tool for incorporating external information. We extend this paradigm by treating organic (non-sponsored) content as a first-class component in the retrieval process and embedding it directly into the mechanism design objective. Finally, reserve prices are a classical tool for revenue optimization in auctions [ 24 , 26 ], with substantial follow-up work on learning reserve prices from samples or historical data [ 5, 9, 6, 16 ]. While these works derive reserves from value distributions or bidding data, our reserve prices arise endogenously from a quality-adjusted welfare formulation: each advertiser is screened based on its marginal contribution relative to the organic baseline, simultaneously preserving output quality and guaranteeing a revenue lower bound.

4 Experiments 

We evaluate the proposed mechanisms across multiple advertiser scenarios. At each segment, we first run the auction to select the winning content, then inject it into the LLM prompt to generate the next segment, continuing the response while naturally incorporating the selected content. In our experiments, we approximate the organic content using LLM-generated concise, factual summaries in a Wikipedia-like style, with each round conditioned on previously generated outputs. Full details on the prompts and LLM are provided in Appendix E. 

Setup. We consider outputs consisting of three sentences. The single-allocation mechanism treats each sentence as a separate segment and conducts one auction per sentence, while the multi-allocation auction treats all three sentences as a single segment and conducts one auction for the entire output. We evaluate six mechanisms in total. The first two are variants of our quality-preserving single-allocation auction (Algorithm 1): QP w/ repl. , which allows repeated selection of the same ad across segments with replacement, and QP w/o repl. , which requires distinct ads at each segment without replacement. 5 Conclusion 

We study mechanism design for LLM advertising and propose a quality-preserving auction framework that integrates organic content into the RAG pipeline and uses an endogenous reserve price to admit only welfare-improving ads. On this screened model, we develop a KL-regularized single-allocation mechanism with Myerson payments and a screened VCG multi-allocation mechanism. Both achieve dominant-strategy incentive compatibility and individual rationality. Experiments across different scenarios show consistent gains in revenue per ad, relevance, and social welfare, while preserving semantic fidelity to no-ad responses. These results demonstrate that effective monetization of LLM systems need not compromise output quality. Several directions remain open for future work. First, the organic welfare function can be learned from platform objectives and user interaction data, rather than being specified a priori. This includes estimating how users trade off relevance, informativeness, and intrusiveness, and using these signals to adaptively shape the reserve price and the resulting revenue–quality trade-off across contexts. Second, incorporating budget constraints and repeated interactions would better capture real-world dynamics, as advertisers face spending limits and platforms operate over multiple rounds. Finally, developing richer measures of output quality beyond semantic similarity may further improve alignment between monetization and user experience, for example by incorporating factors such as factual accuracy or user engagement. 12 References
